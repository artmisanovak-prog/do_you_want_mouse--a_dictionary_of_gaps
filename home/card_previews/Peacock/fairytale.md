# ABOUT THE PEACOCK

## Eye after eye, peacock after peacock – and no one knows why.

Once upon a time, there was a peacock who had so many eyes on his tail that they started looking back at him.  

Not he at the world, but the world at him – through his own feathers.  

And that was a problem.



Because when you look at the world through yourself, everything looks like you.  

And the peacock began to think that the whole world was a peacock.  

That trees were peacocks, just without feathers.  

That clouds were peacocks who had dissolved.  

And that a mouse was just a peacock who forgot to unfold.



And so he began counting peacocks.  

Not by the number of legs, but by the number of eyes.  

Whoever had more than one eye was suspicious.  

Whoever had fewer than one was a prophet.



One day he met a lemur who only looked sideways.  

The peacock paused: “That’s a peacock who turned inward.”  

Then he met a tomcat who burped dots.  

“That’s a peacock who swallowed punctuation.”



And when he met a doe who had walked through fire, he said:  

“That’s a peacock who burned himself out.”



And so a new theory was born:  

Every animal is a peacock in a different stage of decay.  

And every eye is just a mirror trying to forget what it saw.



The peacock began tearing out his feathers.  

Not because he wanted to be less beautiful.  

But because he wanted to be less peacock.



And when he had only one eye left, he finally saw:  

That he was not a peacock.  

That he was a fairy tale.

 And that

 a fairy tale cannot be unfolded like a tail.
THE END
