A CROOKED FAIRY TALE – A STRAIGHT TESTIMONY

Written by a hand that didn't want to explain anything.

These fairy tales don't tell stories the way they should.
Instead of magic, there is misunderstanding.
Instead of good and evil — a strange calm between them.
The animals speak here, but not to entertain us.
They say what people cannot say out loud.
Stories that happened more than they were invented —
and perhaps are still happening.
They just can't be seen from the usual angle.

A CROOKED FAIRY TALE ⬇️

Such a small, strange, crooked
fairy tale about a mouse,
that may not even be a fairy tale,
but something
that happened.

And in that world there are other animals too
— a stork, a peacock, a cat, a goat…
but all of it is
half a dream,
half a joke,
half an interpretation
and half completely off

ABOUT THE MOUSE
and what happens when you DON'T notice her

Once upon a time — yet not so very long ago.
And not exactly nearby, but not that far away either.

There lived a mouse.
She was small, the way mice
usually are.
And she had two very large ears,
which is also nothing unusual for a mouse.

She wasn't particularly fast,
but not completely slow either.
She wasn't stupid,
but not exceptionally clever.

At first glance,
an ordinary, everyday mouse.

But not — normal.
She noticed things that others overlooked.

For instance, that a sentence sometimes catches in the air.
Or that the peacock,
when nobody is looking,
counts his eyes.
Or that the stork
sometimes just
lands on a chimney
and forgets
whom he was supposed to bring.

And this mouse
began to write things down.
Not on paper.
But somehow.
Into silence.
Into corners.
Into the cracks between words,
where nobody usually reads.

And then one day she noticed
that animals were beginning to gather
around her.

Not in a line.
Not in a circle.
Just…
somehow.

And each one was carrying something
it didn't explain.

The hare had burnt ears
and smelled of smoke.
The cat was choking with laughter,
because it had swallowed a full stop.
The elephant was carrying cherries in its trunk,
that would never ripen.
And the lemur wasn't looking at anything,
only to the side,
where there was nothing.

The mouse said nothing.
But she noted everything down.
And then one day
she asked the first question.

Not a big one.
Not an important one.
Just that one:

"Do you want a mouse?"

And that was not a question
asking
about herself.
It was a question
asking
about everything.

About meaning.
About language.
About fear.
About
what anyone will make of us.

And also about
whether you will let this fairy tale
be told to you
to the end.
Or squeak and run.
Or fold it differently.

Because from that moment,
it was no longer just a mouse.

It was a key.
And a paddle.
And a trap.
And the end of the introduction.
But the beginning of something else.

..perhaps..
