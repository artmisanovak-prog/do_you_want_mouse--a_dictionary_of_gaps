# 🪿 GEESE – Card sample

## Core dysfunction

**Collective singular and loud self-deception.** The team consists of individuals who are too occupied with their own "I" (their opinions, position, need to be heard), but this "I" is empty and uncertain. Instead of cooperating, they merely "fly in a circle" – repeatedly stating their positions, amplifying each other, and creating the illusion of movement and discussion, while in reality heading nowhere.

Their communication ("me me mememe meme me") is a "manifesto of identity in decay" – an attempt at self-definition that fails because content and mutual listening are missing. It is a team full of individual stars who, instead of harmony, create only a cacophony of ego.

## 📘 Fairy tale

# ABOUT THE GEESE 
## who spoke "me me mememe meme me"

In the north, where words break against the wind and stories are born from mist, there lived a flock of geese.  
Large.
White.
Ordinary.
At least in their own opinion.
And opinions, those they had in endless supply.
And these particular geese compensated for it by doing word carrying.
Only..
Each of them carried one "me".  
And none of all those "me"s were sure whether they were really "me", or just an echo of another "me".
And so the geese didn't fly in a wedge.  
They flew in a circle.  
And screamed:
"Me me mememe meme me!"
Those who heard them were half-confused. 
Some thought it was singing.  
others, that it was a disorder.  
But in reality it was - a manifesto of identity in decay.

One goose, her name was probably Me, maybe MeMe, once stopped and said:

"I am me, but only when me is heard. When me is not heard, I am just meme. And when you hear me too much, I am mememe. And when you hear me constantly, I am meme me. And then I don't know who I am."

The other geese nodded their necks as if in real panic.  
"Me me mememe meme me!"  
That wasn't a scream.  
That was a grammatical crisis.

Once a small curious mouse came.  
She asked:  
"Who are you?"  
And the geese answered:
"Me."  
"Me."  
"Me."  
"Me."  
"Me."  
"Me."  
"Me me mememe meme me."
The mouse laughed.  
"Aha. Collective singular. I know that."
She didn't add anything more.
And ran away. 
And didn't say where.

Since then it is said that when someone repeats "me" so long that it sounds like "mememe",  
perhaps they are not seeking attention.  
Perhaps they are seeking white geese.
Perhaps their me was not heard.

Geese are not stupid.  
They are just loud attempts at self-deception.

## 🎯 3 diagnostic questions

1. **Q1 (Circular flight):** "Where in our team do we 'fly in a circle'? Where do we discuss, repeat our positions, amplify each other, but in reality reach no decision, progress, or deeper understanding?"

2. **Q2 (Grammatical crisis):** "When everyone screams 'me', no one hears 'we'. How does 'grammatical crisis' manifest in our team – the inability to find common language, to move from individual positions to collective action? Where does our communication resemble 'me me mememe'?"

3. **Q3 (Seeking the gosling):** "The gosling with 'suitcase full of interest' asked 'Who are you?' and then left. Who or what in our team could play the role of the 'gosling' – external observer, new member, process – that would help us stop screaming 'me' and start actually answering the question 'Who are we (as a team)?'"

## 🧠 Sample angles (3 of 12)

### 1. ANGLE OF COLLECTIVE SINGULAR ("Who are you?" – "Me. Me. Me.")

**How it manifests in team:**
The team is not a group, but a cluster. To the question "Who are you?" they cannot answer "we", only a choir of individual "me"s. Everyone speaks for themselves, but no one for the whole. Result: paralysis. Instead of harmony, a cacophony of ego emerges.

**What to do about it (sample solution):**
Introduce ritual **"we-check"** at start of meetings: everyone must begin sentence with word "We are…" and describe shared goal or state. This trains collective consciousness and responsibility.

---

### 2. ANGLE OF CIRCULAR FLIGHT (Movement without direction)

**How it manifests in team:**
They don't fly in a wedge (toward a goal), but in a circle. Projects spin endlessly in analyses, scope adjustments, infinite discussions. Meetings where the same thing is discussed without moving anything forward. All energy goes to maintaining illusion of activity, not achieving goal.

**What to do about it (sample solution):**
Introduce strict rule **"out of the circle"**: At end of every meeting it must be clear:
1. What was decided?
2. Who will do it?
3. By when?
Without this, the meeting is considered ineffective circling.

---

### 3. ANGLE OF LOUD ATTEMPT AT SELF-DECEPTION

**How it manifests in team:**
The final sentence of the fairy tale is key: "Geese are not stupid. They are just loud attempts at self-deception." The louder the team screams about their success or unity, the more likely they doubt it. Culture of toxic positivity: "Everything is great!" we scream to convince ourselves, because deep down we know it's not.

**What to do about it (sample solution):**
Replace loud declarations with silent questioning. Instead of "We are a great team!" ask **"What would help us be a better team?"** Introduce ritual where it is allowed to say unpleasant things without punishment.

## 🛠️ Workshop (structure)

### Workshop: "FROM CIRCLE TO WEDGE" – 90 minutes

**Goal:** Identify patterns of "circular flight" and "collective singular" in the team, break the cycle of empty screaming "me" and begin building shared "wedge" – clear direction and rules of real cooperation.

**Part 1: Diagnosis of our flock (30 min)**
- Introduction, reading the fairy tale
- Individual writing: **RED CARD** (where we circle) and **BLUE CARD** (what I scream my "me")
- Creating image: circle of red cards on floor, pile of blue in center

**Part 2: Breaking the circle and finding direction (40 min)**
- Group work **"From word carrier to navigator"** – analysis of circular topic and proposal of "maneuver out of circle"
- Group work **"From Me to We"** – reformulating individual "me"s into shared "we" and proposing rule of cooperation

**Part 3: Forming our wedge (20 min)**
- Presentation and selection of one "maneuver out of circle" and one rule of cooperation
- Joint drawing of wedge: tip = goal, wings = maneuver + rule, feathers = signatures of team members

## 📊 Diagnostic matrix

| Dominant angle | Diagnosis | Intervention ("From circle to wedge") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Collective singular** | Team is a set of loud individual "me"s that cannot create "we". | **"We Day":** Entire day forbidden to use words "I" and "my". Only "we", "our", "team". |
| **Circular flight** | Team spins in cycles of discussions without decisions and progress. Much movement, no direction. | **"Wedge navigator":** Appoint person who watches agenda and when circling says: "Now we are in circle. I propose decision X." |
| **Grammatical crisis** | Communication is full of empty, repeating words and phrases. Linguistic noise emerges. | **"Word detox":** For one week ban 3 emptiest buzzwords of team. Replace with concrete descriptions. |
| **Loud self-deception** | Team deludes itself about successes or unity through loud rhetoric that masks real problems. | **"Ritual of silence":** At start of retrospective 3 minutes silence, everyone writes one unpleasant fact. Then they are read. |
| **Need to be heard** | Members fight for attention, feel they exist only when speaking. Meetings are full of shouting over each other. | **"Circle of hearing":** Use talking object. Only the one holding it speaks. Before passing, summarize what previous said. |

## 💡 Why Geese are key?

This is a card of group dynamics and ego. It perfectly complements the system: while **Peacock** is about self-centeredness of individual, **Geese** show what happens when several such individuals attempt to create a team. It is about failure of cooperation at basic level. About meetings where everyone speaks and no one listens, about projects without leader and teams without cohesion.

**Geese are a critical piece of the puzzle** – they connect individual pathologies into group dynamics and show how dysfunctional individuals mutually reinforce each other into dysfunctional whole. It is a master card of team therapy.
