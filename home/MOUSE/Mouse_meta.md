## Key to angles — or How the Mouse reads fairy tales

—

The Mouse takes notes in silence.
In the gaps and cracks between words.

She doesn't write what happened. She writes **how** we look at it.

Because the Mouse discovered long ago that one fairy tale is not one story.
It is twelve stories at once.
And each of them reveals a different truth about the person reading.

—

**This is not a diagnostic table.**
This is a **guide to reading like a mouse**.

—

### WHAT THE MOUSE SEES WHEN READING ANSWERS

The Mouse doesn't measure correctness. She doesn't measure depth.
The Mouse assigns.

In every answer to three simple questions (Meaning, Engagement, Application)
she looks for the **core** — the way the speaker connected to the story.

—

**1. ABSURD ANGLE** — Logic in reverse

*Core:* Looks for contradiction, paradox, the place where the world stopped making sense.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone was not satisfied with an explanation. Here, someone needed the world to be consistently inconsistent."
*Trace in answer:* Emphasis on nonsense, on the collapse of rules, on the absurdity of the system.

—

**2. LINGUISTIC ANGLE** — Playing with words

*Core:* Hears a word, a sentence, a rhythm. Form is content.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone doesn't distinguish between what is said and how it is said. For them, the way of expressing something is the highest message."
*Trace in answer:* Quoting a specific phrase, analysing rhythm, wordplay, onomatopoeia.

—

**3. IRONIC ANGLE** — Turning things into the ridiculous

*Core:* Sees the gap between intention and result, between pose and reality.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone knows that things are not what they seem. And it pleases them to know it before everyone else."
*Trace in answer:* Exposing hypocrisy, awkwardness, self-deception. Light sarcasm, a bitter smile.

—

**4. PHILOSOPHICAL ANGLE** — Existential questions

*Core:* Looks for universal truth, the essence of being, the meaning behind the meaning.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone is not answering the question. They are answering the question behind the question. And perhaps not even that."
*Trace in answer:* A general truth, a metaphysical tone, emphasis on essence rather than plot.

—

**5. SURREALIST ANGLE** — Free associations

*Core:* Connects the unconnectable. Thinks in images, not arguments.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone doesn't interpret. Here, someone lets the story spread through them. And then writes down what surfaced."
*Trace in answer:* Bizarre connections, dreamlike logic, an image without explanation. Beauty that doesn't quite fit.

—

**6. NAIVE ANGLE** — A child's logic

*Core:* Asks simply. Answers directly. Doesn't add anything.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone doesn't need to be clever. They need to be truthful. And that is much harder."
*Trace in answer:* Straightforward moral, simple emotion, a clean impression without rationalisation.

—

**7. INTRO (INTROSPECTIVE) ANGLE** — Inner monologue

*Core:* Projection of their own experience. The story is a pretext for self-knowledge.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone is not reflecting on the fairy tale. They are reflecting on themselves through the fairy tale. And they are willing to admit it."
*Trace in answer:* Personal experience, emotion, memory. "I feel…", "It touched me…", "It reminded me of…"

—

**8. GENERAL (CONCRETE) ANGLE** — What you see is what it is

*Core:* Sticks to the facts. Plot is plot. No need to look for hidden meanings.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone trusts what is written. Not because they are naive. Because they know there is often nothing beneath the surface."
*Trace in answer:* Retelling the plot, stating facts, resistance to interpretation.

—

**9. ANALOGICAL (CULTURAL) ANGLE** — Relationships, archetypes

*Core:* Looks for patterns. Compares to something already known.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone believes there is nothing new under the sun. Every story has already been told — just in different clothing."
*Trace in answer:* Reference to another story, myth, archetype, historical parallel.

—

**10. ASSOCIATIVE ANGLE** — Quick connections

*Core:* Thinking through personal associations, unexpected connections.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone doesn't think linearly. They think in networks. Every word is a node, every sentence triggers ten more."
*Trace in answer:* A chain of associations, digressions, personal anecdotes, jumping thoughts.

—

**11. METAPHORICAL (SYMBOLIC) ANGLE** — Image → meaning

*Core:* Everything is a symbol of something greater. Nothing is just what it is.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone reads the fairy tale like a cipher. Every animal, every object, every movement is a key to something unspoken."
*Trace in answer:* Decoding symbols, searching for hidden meaning, converting image into abstraction.

—

**12. GROTESQUE ANGLE** — Comedy pushed to the extreme

*Core:* Sees the exaggerated, the caricatured, the darkly comic. Laughter that scratches.
*Mouse notes:* "Here, someone knows that tragedy and comedy are sisters. And that the best way to talk about pain is to turn it into a circus."
*Trace in answer:* Emphasis on exaggeration, awkwardness, dark humour, caricature.

—

### WHAT DOES THE MOUSE DO WITH THIS?

The Mouse doesn't judge. The Mouse doesn't say which angle is better.
The Mouse **counts**.

—

**1. SHE WRITES DOWN ALL THE ANSWERS.**

Three questions. Every team member. Anonymously.

—

**2. SHE ASSIGNS ONE ANGLE TO EACH ANSWER.**

The most fitting one. Not the only one — the most prominent.

—

**3. SHE COUNTS HOW MANY TIMES EACH ANGLE APPEARED.**

And suddenly the fairy tale is no longer about animals.
It is about the team.

—

**4. SHE IDENTIFIES PATTERNS.**

*OVERLOADED ANGLES* — The team keeps looking the same way. It has blinkers on. What it sees, it sees well. What it doesn't see, doesn't exist.
*DEFICIENT ANGLES* — Blind spots. Perspectives the team doesn't use, even though it urgently needs them.
*BALANCED ANGLES* — A healthy core. This is where a bridge can be built.

—

**5. SHE PROPOSES ACTION.**

*The overloaded* are given a task to think differently. (An Ironic must spend one day looking only for poetry.)
*The deficient* are sent on a mandatory excursion. (Speak only in metaphors for a whole week.)
*The balanced* are used as interpreters. (Translate the problem from the overloaded language into the deficient one.)

—

### WHY DOES THE MOUSE DO THIS?

The Mouse doesn't want to be clever.
The Mouse doesn't want to be useful.
The Mouse just takes notes.

But when she takes notes long enough and carefully enough,
she begins to see things in her notes
that nobody else sees.

Not because she has better eyes.
Because she looks **elsewhere**.

—

**This is the key to angles.**
It is not a table. It is not a guide.
It is a mouse, watching.

And asking:

—

**"Do You Want a Mouse?"**
