## Core dysfunction

**Paralysis from ambiguity and false certainty.** The team is trapped between alternatives it cannot distinguish ("hay vs. straw"), because it lacks clear criteria, own judgment, or courage to choose. Instead it accepts empty symbols ("labels") and becomes a "guide" that merely looks like it knows where it's going. Thus it maintains the system in illusion of direction, while in reality wandering.

This is a dysfunction of lost contact with reality, inability to distinguish essential from non-essential, and creating empty rituals that replace real decision-making.

## 📘 Fairy tale

## ABOUT THE DONKEY who went left, but thought he was going right

The donkey was born between two piles.
One was hay.
The other straw.
And no one told him which was which.

"Left," said someone.
"Right," said another.
"Hay," said a voice.
"Straw," said a shadow.

The donkey looked.
Both piles looked the same.
One a bit softer.
One a bit louder.
But otherwise? Chaos.

"For one a dog's thing," said the voice.
"For another a donkey's thing," added another.
And the donkey thought:
"Who am I? A dog's thing, or a donkey's thing?"

He went left.
Then turned around.
And went right.
But it looked the same.
Earth was earth.
Pile was pile.
And rules? Invisible.

He met a mouse.
She was writing down what was happening.
"Where are you going?" she asked.
"I don't know," said the donkey.
"Left, right, hay, straw. Everything mixes together for me."

The mouse noted:
Donkey – confused, but consistent.

"And what are you looking for?"
"Correctness," said the donkey.
"And what is that?"
"That which is not said aloud, but everyone expects it."

The mouse thought.
And then handed him a label.
On one side was written "hay".
On the other "straw".
But the script was the same.

The donkey hung it around his neck.
And went on.
Left.
Right.
Hay.
Straw.

And when someone stopped him and asked:
"Where are you going?"
He said:
"According to the label."
"And what is on it?"
"That which you think is there."

And so he became a guide.
Not one who knows.
But one who goes.
And when someone got lost,
they went after the donkey.
Because even though he doesn't know,
he looks like he knows.

And when someone asked what is a "donkey's thing",
the mouse said:
"That's when you go on, even when you don't know where."

## 🎯 3 diagnostic questions

1. **Q1 (Analysis of paralysis):** "Why couldn't the donkey distinguish hay from straw, even though the difference is essential? What prevented him from using his own senses (taste, touch) and relying on his own experience?"

2. **Q2 (Identification of illusion):** "Where in our team/process do we create 'labels' – empty tags, rituals, reports or buzzwords that are meant to cover that we actually don't know which way to go?"

3. **Q3 (Transformation):** "What would have to happen for our team to become a true guide – that is, to know where it's going, and not just 'look like it knows'? How to transform 'donkey's thing' (going on without knowing) into 'mouse's thing' (going with awareness of goal)?"

## 🧠 Sample angles (3 of 12)

### 1. ANGLE OF LOST SENSORICS (Inability to taste)

**How it manifests in team:**
Team doesn't gather own data, doesn't ask users, doesn't test hypotheses, but relies on reports, surveys and expert opinions. Loses direct contact with "taste" of the problem. The donkey forgot he has tongue and smell – doesn't rely on own experience, only on foreign descriptions ("voice", "shadow").

**What to do about it (sample solution):**
Introduce rule of **"tastings"**: Before every big decision, at least one team member must gain direct, unfiltered experience – talk to customer, try using own product, spend a day in the field.

---

### 2. ANGLE OF CONTRADICTORY INSTRUCTIONS (Voice vs. shadow)

**How it manifests in team:**
Team receives mixed signals from leadership, different departments have contradictory priorities, strategy changes direction according to who happens to speak at the meeting. The donkey is bombarded with advice from anonymous authorities – "left/right", "hay/straw" – and is paralyzed.

**What to do about it (sample solution):**
Establish **"unified command"** for each project. If instructions are contradictory, demand their confrontation and clarification at common forum. Record final decisions and reasons.

---

### 3. ANGLE OF EMPTY LABEL (Symbol instead of substance)

**How it manifests in team:**
Team creates empty tags, buzzwords, complex diagrams or rituals (like agile ceremonies without content), which mask absence of real understanding. The mouse gave the donkey a label with words "hay" and "straw", but the script was the same – the label is an empty symbol meant to create illusion of distinction.

**What to do about it (sample solution):**
Test **"labels"** with simple question: "What specifically does this word/process mean in practice for our today's task?" If cannot be answered concretely and simply, it's an empty label. Rewrite it into language of actions.

## 🛠️ Workshop (structure)

### Workshop: "BETWEEN TWO PILES" – 90 minutes

**Goal:** Identify areas of ambiguity and paralysis in team, replace empty "labels" with concrete criteria, and restore ability to distinguish essential.

**Part 1: Diagnosis of our wandering (30 min)**
- Introduction, reading the fairy tale
- Individual reflection: **HAY** (activity with clear value), **STRAW** (activity with illusory value), **LABEL** (empty word/phrase)
- Creating boards "HAY", "STRAW", "LABELS", silent reading

**Part 2: Tasting and relabeling (40 min)**
- Group work **"Thumb test"** – rewriting selected "labels" into concrete actions
- Group work **"Distinguisher"** – proposal of simple test that reveals whether activity from "straw" is truly useless

**Part 3: Commitment and new rules (20 min)**
- Selection of one label to rewrite and one activity to test
- Formulation of **"Distinction Protocol"** – simple rule for testing new tasks

## 📊 Diagnostic matrix

| Dominant angle | Diagnosis | Intervention ("Cure of donkeyness") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lost sensorics** | Team lives second-hand. Lost direct contact with reality. | **"Day of direct experience":** Every member must spend 2 hours in direct contact with reality (customer, production, support). |
| **Contradictory instructions** | Team is paralyzed by many-headed leadership. | **"Map of decision-making powers":** Explicitly draw who is responsible for what. |
| **Existential crisis** | Team doesn't know who it is and where it belongs. Loss of self-confidence. | **"Identity workshop":** Answer questions: What makes us unique? What will we never do? |
| **Empty labels** | Communication is full of jargon and empty phrases. | **"Scratch-out Friday":** Select 3 buzzwords and don't use them all Friday. |
| **Guide with impostor syndrome** | Leadership gives impression of omniscience, but team feels it is led blindly. | **"Meeting of 'I don't know'":** Leader at start of meeting admits one thing they don't know, and asks team for help. |

## 💡 Why Donkey is key?

Perfectly complements the system. While **Tomcat** prematurely closes and **Butterfly** aestheticizes hunger, **Donkey** cannot even begin to eat. This is the card of **paralysis by analysis, bureaucracy, and loss of healthy common sense**. It is about teams that spin in circles of consultations, create stacks of documents, but are incapable of simple, courageous decision. It is the antidote to illusion of progress.

Donkey teaches us that movement without direction is not progress. That empty symbols and labels don't replace own experience and courage to distinguish. And that we become true guides not by looking like we know, but by tasting, asking, and admitting when we don't know.
