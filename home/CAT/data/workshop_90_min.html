🐈‍⬛ Workshop: "Half an Hour of Silence"

90 minutes · Facilitator's guide · CAT card

0 / 9 steps

📋 Prepare before the workshop

  • Stopwatch / timer (or use this tool)
  • Flip chart or whiteboard + markers
  • Paper for each participant
  • Optional: a physical ladybird as a talking stick
Part 1 — Diagnosis · 25 minutes
Step 1: Reading the fairy tale
5 min

The facilitator reads the whole fairy tale out loud. Participants only listen — no notes, no phones.

💡 Read slowly. Pauses are intentional. Don't rush to the next step.
5:00
Step 2: Individual reflection
10 min

Each participant writes independently in response to two questions:

  1. One situation when someone talked over me or my thought was never completed.
  2. One situation when I talked over someone or finished their sentence for them.
💡 Remind participants: they are writing for themselves — they will not read it aloud to the whole group (only in small groups).
10:00
Step 3: Sharing in small groups
10 min

Split into groups of 3–4 people. Share situations according to the rule:

Rule for this round:
Description only: "It happened… I felt…"
No judgement. No "but". No advice.

The facilitator moves between groups and checks that people are sharing, not discussing.

10:00
Part 2 — Experiment and new rules · 45 minutes
Step 4a: Experiment – phase A
15 min

The facilitator presents one specific current problem the team is facing (prepare this in advance).

Rule for phase A:
Nobody may finish another person's sentence.
You may ask questions, paraphrase — but not complete "and therefore…", "so what you mean is…"
The facilitator enforces this strictly and names interruptions: "That was a full stop on Sarah's sentence."
15:00
Step 4b: Experiment – phase B
15 min

Continue discussing the same problem with a new rule:

Rule for phase B:
Banned words: "but", "however", "although", "yet"
Allowed replacements: "and at the same time…", "I'd add…", "I also see it this way…", "what if…"

Write the banned words on the whiteboard. Each time someone uses one — note it, and at the end name how many times it happened.

15:00
Step 5: Reflection on the experiment
15 min

Whole group together. The facilitator asks questions — writes answers on the whiteboard:

  1. What was different from a normal meeting?
  2. What was hard? What surprised you?
  3. What ideas emerged that would otherwise have been "swallowed by the cat"?
💡 Don't rush. Silence is also an answer — hold it for at least 10 seconds.
15:00

10 min

10:00
Part 3 — Action commitment · 20 minutes
Step 7 + 8: Agreement and Guardian of the Ladybirds
10 min

Agreement on the manifesto: Everyone says "I agree" out loud, or signs the printed manifesto. A physical commitment is stronger.

Appointing the Guardian of the Ladybirds: Choose 2 people for the next month (they can rotate between meetings).

The Guardian's task:
Observe communication during meetings.
At the end of each meeting, say: "Today I noticed X moments when…"
No blame — only naming.
10:00
Step 9: Closing ritual
10 min

Go around the room. Each person completes this sentence out loud — in their own words, without interruption:

"To stop being the cat, next time I will…"

The facilitator goes last. Nobody comments on anyone else's answer.

💡 If someone doesn't know, skip them and come back at the end. Silence is fine.
10:00

🐾 Workshop complete

The team had 90 minutes. Now it has a manifesto, a Guardian, and a different experience of what a meeting can be.
See you in a month — what changed?
