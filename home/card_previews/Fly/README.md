# 🪰 FLY

**Core dysfunction:**
Normalized chaos and blindness to own problem. The team (or environment) has been struggling with a dysfunction for so long that it has become common and ceased to be perceived. It became an invisible part of the landscape. The breaking point comes when an external, non-judgmental element ("fly") appears, for whom this "mess" is on the contrary ideal environment. This element by its mere existence makes the true nature of the problem visible and forces the team to reflection.

### <a name="fairytale"></a>📘 FAIRY TALE

## About the mulberry and the fly

*or how a mess became paradise*

Once – but easily also this afternoon –
there stood in a courtyard an old mulberry.
She wasn't beautiful.
She wasn't noble.
She wasn't practical.
Sweet, dark mold fell from her for years,
which colored the pavement, shoes, and conscience.

Under the mulberry lived the mess-makers.
Not the dramatically scattered ones.
Those would have style.
These mess-makers were quiet.
They left things where they remained.
The mug that "I'll still use".
Papers that "will come in handy".
Stains that "will dry and disappear".
They didn't disappear.

The sun baked.
The mulberry dripped.
Remains rotted.
And the world under the tree slowly changed its state.

Then a fly flew in.
She wasn't exceptional.
She wasn't heroic.
She was perfectly fly-like.

She flew in from a mess.
Someone else's.
Not hers.

She circled above the courtyard.
Caught the scent.
Earth.
Sweetness.
Decay.
Possibility.

And landed.

For the mess-makers: catastrophe.
For the fly: smorgasbord of existence.

She sat on a spot under the mulberry,
which formed slowly,
drop by drop,
without evil intent,
without plan,
without witnesses.

She buzzed.
Not loudly.
Just enough to be heard in the silence.

The mess-makers raised their heads.

"Ugh."

And at that moment they noticed:
stains,
stickiness,
scent that is not a scent,
life that got into motion.

They waved their hands.
Cursed.
Looked for a rag.
Looked for a culprit.

The fly buzzed contentedly.
That sound wasn't loud.
Just couldn't be ignored.

One mess-maker waved his hand.
Second swore.
Third lifted his shoe from the ground – and it stayed stuck to the floor.
Fourth noticed for the first time that a stain is not a stain.
It's geology.

The fly ran a few centimeters further.

And suddenly they saw.
Not the fly.
Themselves.

The sticky metaphysics of reality stuck to their attention.
Steps left imprints.
Imprints created layers.
Layers created history.
History created trap.

"This can't be cleaned up," said one.
"How did this happen?" said second.

No one answered.

The fly ate her fill.
For her it was paradise.
For them it was finally a visible problem.
Not because it was new.
But because it started buzzing.

And in that buzzing was heard:
procrastination,
fatigue,
tomorrow,
that never came.

One lifted a box.
Under it was another world.
Second opened a window.
The air didn't know it was allowed inside.
Third carried out a bag.
It was heavier than expected.
Fourth for the first time didn't decide to sit down.

No one promised anything.
No one transformed into a better person.
Just for the first time they saw the shape of the problem.

The fly sat.
It wasn't her mess, but it was for her.
The mess was home.
Where others saw ruin,
she saw abundance.
Where they felt shame,
she felt the warmth of the world.
Where they began cleaning,
she began feasting.

And the mess-makers?
They cleaned.
And the more they cleaned,
the more they realized,
that the mess wasn't born today.
It formed drop by drop.
Decision by decision.
Procrastination by procrastination.

And the fly?
She buzzed.
Not as reproach.
As commentary.

She ate her fill.
Warmed up.
Then flew away.
Because paradise is not a place
where you stay.
It's a place
that no longer needs you.

Under the mulberry remained the courtyard.
Cleaner.
Slower.
A bit shy.

The mess-makers stood in silence,
which wasn't empty.

And somewhere high above them
circled a fly,
seeking another foreign mess,
which could be transformed
into a perfect world.

Because for a fly, shit is not a problem.
It's an opportunity.
And the world is full of opportunities.

The End.

### <a name="core"></a>🎯 CORE DYSFUNCTION AND 3 QUESTIONS

**Three diagnostic questions:**

1. **Q1:** Why did the mess-makers stop noticing their own courtyard, while on the contrary the fly immediately found paradise in it?
2. **Q2:** Where in our team do we have our own "mulberry" – a place, process or habit that has been dysfunctional for so long that we stopped noticing it? Who could be our "fly"?
3. **Q3:** What would have to happen for us ourselves to be able to see our "courtyard" through the eyes of a fly – not as our own failure, but as an environment that can be changed?

### <a name="angles"></a>🧠 12 WORK ANGLES (sample)

**1. Absurdist angle** (paradoxical reality)
- Q1: "Mess is relative. What is catastrophe for one, is paradise for another."
- Q2: "I was struck by the sentence: 'Fly in the mess'. It's a perfect absurd image."
- Q3: "Look at the biggest problem and try to describe it as paradise for someone else."

**2. Linguistic angle** (speech of silence and buzzing)
- Q1: "The story is about how a problem becomes audible. The mess-makers lived in silence until buzzing sounded."
- Q2: "The fly buzzed. Not loudly. Just enough to be heard in the silence."
- Q3: "Look for 'buzzing' in the team – quiet signals that something is wrong."

**3. Ironic angle** (when solution becomes problem)
- Q1: "The irony is that the mess-makers would most like to kill the fly that made their problem visible."
- Q2: "For the fly, the mess wasn't hers, but it was for her."
- Q3: "Ask: Who or what profits from this mess?"

### <a name="workshop"></a>📋 90 MINUTE WORKSHOP (structure)

**Part 1: Diagnosis – Who are we in this story? (30 min)**
- Introduction, reading the fairy tale, individual writing (mulberry and fly cards), joint board.

**Part 2: Deep work – How to become a fly? (40 min)**
- Exercise "View from the fly" (describe problem as paradise) and practice of "buzzing" in pairs.

**Part 3: Action plan – What will be our buzzing? (20 min)**
- Selection of specific "buzzing" to implement, formulation of "Fly Protocol".

### <a name="creative-mode"></a>🎨 CREATIVE MODE – SAMPLES

The card principle can be flipped into positive tone and used in marketing, design or corporate culture.

**1. For copywriting: "Seekers of hidden opportunities"**
- **Insight:** Fly sees paradise where others see mess.
- **Usage:** Campaign for company that finds solutions where others see dead end.
- **Example:** "While others see disorder, we see smorgasbord. Specialists in discovering opportunities."

**2. For product design: "Design that buzzes"**
- **Insight:** Small, unignorable impulse triggers big change.
- **Usage:** Product with built-in "buzzing" that gently alerts to overlooked things.
- **Example:** App that once daily sends question: "What did you forget today?"

**3. For visual identity: "Aesthetics of sticky reality"**
- **Insight:** Beauty arises from decay, from layers of time, from postponed things.
- **Usage:** Visual style working with textures, drops, stains.
- **Example:** Logo where every stain has its story – fly sitting on imperfect composition.

### <a name="matrix"></a>📊 DIAGNOSTIC MATRIX

| Dominant angle | Diagnosis | Intervention |
|---|---|---|
| **Absurdist** | Team got used to absurd state and considers it normal. | Invite external observer to describe what they see. |
| **Linguistic** | Silence about fundamental problems prevails in team. | Introduce "minute of buzzing" at every meeting. |
| **Ironic** | System profits from chaos. | Identify who benefits from the problem and redirect them. |
| **Naive** | Team lost ability to see things for the first time. | Everyone tries role of newcomer and asks "why?". |

### <a name="usage"></a>🚀 HOW TO USE THE CARD

1. Read the **fairy tale** – aloud, together.
2. Ask the **3 diagnostic questions** – everyone answers for themselves.
3. Analyze answers according to **12 angles** – you get team profile.
4. According to prevailing angles select **intervention** from matrix.
5. Conduct **workshop** (90 minutes) for deeper work.
6. Use **creative mode** for positive inspiration.
