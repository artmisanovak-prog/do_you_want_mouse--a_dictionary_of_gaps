## 3 diagnostic questions (for this specific problem)

1. **Q1 (Analysis):** "Why did the cat talk over people and finish their sentences for them? What did it gain — and what did it destroy?"
2. **Q2 (Identification):** "When and who in our team most often plays the role of the 'cat' — that is, prematurely closes discussions or finishes other people's thoughts?"
3. **Q3 (Remedy):** "How can we create a '14 full stops' rule — a mechanism that forces us to let every important thought be fully 'burped out'?"

## Preview of 2 angles out of 12

### 1. 🔚 THE ANGLE OF THE PHYSICAL END OF A SENTENCE
**Problem:** The tyranny of punctuation – emphasis on closure, not on content
- **Team example:** When someone says "so…" or "maybe we could…" and is immediately interrupted with a solution. Or when management pushes for a quick 'action point' before the problem has been properly explored.
- **Solution:** Introduce the 'three dots' rule (…). When someone hints at something, they must have space to develop it. The first person to say 'full stop' has one task only: to summarise what they heard — NOT to add their own conclusion.

### 2. ⏱️ THE ANGLE OF QUASI-EFFICIENCY
**Problem:** The illusion of quick progress
- **Team example:** When a project manager pushes to quickly 'tick off' agenda items without getting to the root of anything. Meetings are short, but problems keep coming back like unburped full stops.
- **Solution:** Measure meeting success not by length, but by the number of open questions at the end. If all items are 'closed', we were probably being cats.
