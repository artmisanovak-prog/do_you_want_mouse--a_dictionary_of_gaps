## How the Mouse reads the CAT

—

The Mouse received a story about a cat
who swallowed two ladybirds and spent half an hour burping full stops.

She read it.
Put it down.
And started taking notes.

—

### WHAT THE MOUSE SEES WHEN READING THE TEAM'S ANSWERS

The team was given three questions.
They answered.
The Mouse assigned.

—

**QUESTION 1 (Meaning):** *"What do you think is the main point or message of this story?"*

**Answer A:** "That the cat destroys communication by finishing everyone's sentences. It's about how speed isn't the same as efficiency."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 3 – Ironic*
(Core: sees the contradiction between the intention — to speed things up — and the result — paralysis.)

**Answer B:** "I don't know, but the phrase 'spent half an hour burping full stops' is brilliant. That rhythm. That specificity. As if time stopped and flowed at the same time."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 2 – Linguistic*
(Core: form is content. The sentence caught their attention, not the point.)

**Answer C:** "That someone has to be the one who closes things down, even when it hurts. The cat is actually a victim of the system."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 7 – Introverted*
(Core: projecting their own experience onto the cat's role.)

**Answer D:** "It's about how every full stop is actually unfinished. Once the cat burped it out, it was no longer his. It belonged to everyone."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 11 – Metaphorical*
(Core: a full stop isn't a full stop — it's a symbol of shared meaning.)

—

**QUESTION 2 (Engagement):** *"Which specific sentence, image or moment caught your attention most and WHY?"*

**Answer A:** "When the mouse says that the ladybirds have unfinished sentence endings on them. That's a precise image of what we carry in a team — things nobody finished, but everyone feels."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 4 – Philosophical*
(Core: searching for a universal truth about the burden of the unfinished.)

**Answer B:** "That passage where 'everyone knew something wasn't the same, but nobody knew what'. That's exactly how I feel after every retrospective."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 10 – Associative*
(Core: personal connection to lived experience, fast jump into practice.)

**Answer C:** "What got me most was that the cat laughed at all of it and kept burping. He's not evil — he's a joker who doesn't know he's causing harm."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 6 – Naive*
(Core: simple, uninterpreted reading of the character. The cat is just a joker.)

**Answer D:** "The ladybird count — exactly 14 full stops. As if someone wanted to add: you see, it's not random, it's a systemic error that can be quantified."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 1 – Absurd*
(Core: sees the paradox in trauma being precisely counted.)

—

**QUESTION 3 (Application):** *"If this story were a guide or piece of advice, what would it tell you to do (or not do) in your work or project life?"*

**Answer A:** "Not to finish other people's sentences. Let people speak. Even if it takes half an hour."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 8 – General*
(Core: sticks to the facts, direct application without metaphor.)

**Answer B:** "To look for 'unswallowed endings' in the team — things that weren't said but are hanging in the air. And then burp them out."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 5 – Surrealist*
(Core: connects the unconnectable — an abstract concept of communication with the image of burping.)

**Answer C:** "It would say not to take ourselves so seriously. Someone has to be the cat, even if it's annoying. At least we're laughing."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 12 – Grotesque*
(Core: sees the comedy even in pathology, laughter as a defence mechanism.)

**Answer D:** "It reminds me of our old manager. He used to interrupt us so often that we stopped talking altogether. Then he complained we had no ideas."
→ **MOUSE ASSIGNED:** *Angle 9 – Analogical*
(Core: looks for a parallel in a specific story, an archetypal situation.)

—

### WHAT DID THE MOUSE READ FROM THIS?

**The team has 12 people.**
The Mouse processed 36 answers.
The result?

—

**OVERLOADED ANGLES** (appeared more than 10×)

• *Ironic (3)* – 11×
• *Introverted (7)* – 10×

→ Diagnosis: The team is clever and self-reflective, but a little cynical. Everyone knows what's going wrong and they enjoy talking about it. Less so about what to actually do.

—

**DEFICIENT ANGLES** (appeared 0–1×)

• *Absurd (1)* – 1×
• *Naive (6)* – 0×
• *Grotesque (12)* – 1×

→ Diagnosis: The team can't be simple. They're afraid of being naive, afraid of absurdity, afraid to laugh at things that hurt. Everything has to make sense. And that is precisely the problem.

—

**BALANCED ANGLES** (2–6×)

• *Linguistic (2)* – 4×
• *Metaphorical (11)* – 5×
• *Associative (10)* – 3×

→ Diagnosis: The team can work with image and language. These are the bridges that can be crossed.

—

### WHAT DID THE MOUSE PROPOSE?

**RULE 1 – FOR THE OVERLOADED**

The Ironics and Introverts are given a task:
At the next meeting they may not evaluate anyone, use irony, or ask "what did the author mean by that".
Instead, their task is to **retell the problem as a fairy tale for a child**.
(Angle 6 – Naive – training the deficit.)

—

**RULE 2 – FOR THE DEFICIENT**

The whole team will spend one week using the **absurd hypothesis rule**.
Every proposal must first be stated in an extremely exaggerated, almost nonsensical form.
Only then do they look for what might be useful in it.
(Angles 1 and 12 – training the deficit.)

—

**RULE 3 – BRIDGES**

People with balanced angles (Language, Metaphor, Association) are given the role of **interpreters**.
Their task is to take one problem from the ironic discourse
and translate it into a language that the absurd and grotesque angle can understand.

—

### WHY DID THE MOUSE SHOW THE CAT?

Because the Cat is a card that seems **simple**.
Teams think: "Ah, it's about interrupting. We'll fix that with a 'no interrupting' rule."

But the Mouse sees that the Cat isn't just about communication.
It's about the relationship to time, about the fear of silence, about the desire to be useful even when it hurts.

And when the Mouse sees **how** the team talks about it,
she sees what is really happening in the team.

—

It's a **glimpse of the mouse's perspective**.
