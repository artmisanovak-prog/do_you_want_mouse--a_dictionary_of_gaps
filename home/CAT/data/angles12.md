# 🐈‍⬛ TOMCAT – 12 angles + metaphor, Q1, Q2, Q3

---

## 1. 🔚 Physical end of sentence  
**Metaphor:** The faster we try to close a sentence, the more of its meanings remain unfinished.  

**Q1:** "The tomcat became a slave to the dot. He believed value lies in ending, not in content."  

**Q2:** "In our meetings, when someone says 'so...' or 'maybe we could...' and is immediately interrupted with a solution. Or when management pushes for a quick 'action point' before the problem was properly explored."  

**Q3:** "Introduce the 'three dots' rule (...). When someone hints at something, they must have space to develop it. The first one to say 'dot' has the task of only summarizing what they heard, NOT adding their own conclusion."

---

## 2. ⏱️ Quasi-efficiency  
**Metaphor:** Quickly ticking off agenda points is like tidying mess into a closet – looks done, but the problem returns.  

**Q1:** "The tomcat thought he was speeding up work. 'Half an hour and done!' But in reality he just replaced a shared process with a monologue."  

**Q2:** "When a project manager or dominant person in the team pushes for quick 'ticking off' agenda points without getting to the essence. Meetings are short, but problems return like unhiccupped dots."  

**Q3:** "Measure meeting success not by length, but by number of open questions at the end. If all points are 'closed', we were probably tomcats."

---

## 3. 🐞 Unswallowable content  
**Metaphor:** Heavy topics we try to swallow eventually come out at the most inappropriate moment.  

**Q1:** "Ladybugs carry 'unswallowed sentence ends'. These are unpleasant, unprocessed, complex aspects of the problem. The tomcat swallowed them, but his body (team) rejected them – hiccupped them back."  

**Q2:** "When we suppress 'elephants in the room', sensitive topics, emotional frustration or fundamental disagreement with approach. We say 'OK, let's move on', but then the topic poisons the atmosphere."  

**Q3:** "Create a 'wall of unswallowed'. Physical/virtual place where people can anonymously write 'unswallowed ends' from discussions. Regularly review and add them to agenda."

---

## 4. 🐀 Observant mouse  
**Metaphor:** Silent voices in the room are like the mouse taking notes – hears more than who speaks, but no one hears her.  

**Q1:** "The mouse is a metaphor for facilitator or silent member. Cannot prevent the tomcat, but observes and saves the answer. Sees dysfunction clearly, but has no power to stop it."  

**Q2:** "When there are introverts in the team or people who need longer time to formulate thoughts. Their voices are silenced by 'tomcat' communication style. Eventually they go silent and frustration grows."  

**Q3:** "Appoint a 'mouse' for every important discussion. Its only role is to observe who speaks, who is interrupted, and at the end share observations without judgment."

---

## 5. 🕳️ Permanent change  
**Metaphor:** Trust is like a spiderweb – once torn, a hole remains even when we try to fix it.  

**Q1:** "Even when the tomcat stopped, 'something wasn't the same'. One-time disruption of communication culture leaves a permanent scar. People subconsciously fear opening their mouths again."  

**Q2:** "After a 'toxic' meeting or period with a dominant boss, the team remains passive even after behavior change, doesn't initiate, waits for instructions. Trust in safe space is gone."  

**Q3:** "Intentionally create 'unfinished sentences'. Leader must start a thought and invite others to finish it. Restore trust by giving up control over the dot."

---

## 6. 🔢 Tyranny of metrics  
**Metaphor:** We count dots but don't see ladybugs – numbers without story are like a map without landscape.  

**Q1:** "The story is obsessed with exact numbers: 2 ladybugs, 14 dots, 30 minutes. This is parody of management reporting where it's about numbers, not story behind them ('why do ladybugs have dots?')."  

**Q2:** "When at retrospective or review only 'done/not done', 'number of tasks' are discussed, but not 'how we feel', 'what we learned', 'what stories are behind the numbers'."  

**Q3:** "Add qualitative story to every quantitative metric. Not 'we completed 14 tasks', but 'what 14 dots did we hiccup and what did it mean for us?'."

---

## 7. 🦾 Violent consensus  
**Metaphor:** Silence in discussion is not agreement, just exhaustion from fighting for word.  

**Q1:** "The tomcat forced consensus by finishing sentences. People stopped speaking because their ending would be the same as his. Created illusion of consensus."  

**Q2:** "When in brainstorm the boss says 'So we all agree on X, right?' and looks around the room. Silence is interpreted as agreement, but in reality it's resignation."  

**Q3:** "Use mechanism of 'silence of consent'. If after presenting decision there is silence, it automatically means DISAGREEMENT. Forces people to actively say 'I agree'."

---

## 8. 🏁 Competition instead of collaboration  
**Metaphor:** Whoever finishes sentence first, wins – but the whole team loses.  

**Q1:** "The tomcat competed: 'who finishes sentence first'. Communication became a race for dominance, not collaboration to find the best thought."  

**Q2:** "In teams where people are rewarded for individual performance, this pattern appears. Interrupting is expression of competition: 'I know better, listen to me'."  

**Q3:** "Introduce 'speaker's round' for key discussions. Use physical object (talking stick). Only the one holding it speaks. Others just actively listen."

---

## 9. 🎁 Unfinished as gift  
**Metaphor:** Unfinished sentence is like an open window – anything new can fly in.  

**Q1:** "The story says: 'Unfinished sentences are all the same.' This is key! Once sentence is finished, it is fixed, dead. Unfinished is alive, full of potential."  

**Q2:** "When team immediately seeks solutions ('let's do A') instead of staying longer in problem definition ('what if it's actually B?'). We fixate on first 'finished' thought."  

**Q3:** "Exercise 'intentionally not finishing'. In brainstorming assign: 'Our problem is like...' and forbid finishing the comparison. Just generate beginnings. Explore which unfinished metaphor is strongest."

---

## 10. 💔 Communication trauma  
**Metaphor:** Scorched earth doesn't grow – after toxic meeting people fear speaking even about weather.  

**Q1:** "The tomcat 'jumped into people's speech' and 'swallowed ladybugs' – these are words of violence. Communication became a traumatizing experience. The team psychologically 'collapsed'."  

**Q2:** "When meetings are source of stress, where people fear speaking because they will be interrupted, humiliated or their idea will be 'swallowed' without respect."  

**Q3:** "Conduct 'audit of communication safety'. Anonymous questionnaire: Do I feel safe expressing disagreement? Am I often interrupted? Are my ideas taken seriously? Set rules based on results."

---

## 11. ⚫ Strong dot  
**Metaphor:** The dot at end of discussion is sometimes a blow that cuts off all further thoughts.  

**Q1:** "'And that a dot is sometimes stronger than a whole sentence.' This is the point! Sometimes formal closure (dot, decision, approval) has more power than entire long discussion. Can be used as weapon."  

**Q2:** "When in long discussions we get lost and then someone authoritatively says 'So decided!' and makes a dot. Team accepts dot out of exhaustion, not conviction."  

**Q3:** "When closing decisions, don't ask 'Is it clear?', but 'Who feels their voices or concerns were not sufficiently heard before this dot?'."

---

## 12. 🍀 Returned luck  
**Metaphor:** Healed trust is like a ladybug returning to where she was almost eaten – fragile, but all the more precious.  

**Q1:** "The story ends with hope: 'The luck of two ladybugs returned.' Even after trauma, recovery is possible. But requires silent understanding ('neither of them said anything') – no need for grand apologies, just change of behavior."  

**Q2:** "When team experiences dysfunctional period, but then without big words the dynamic changes. People cautiously regain trust. This is a fragile process."  

**Q3:** "Establish 'ladybug ritual'. Whenever someone feels they were 'eaten' (interrupted, silenced), they can with silent symbol (e.g. place ladybug on table) signal need to slow down and listen."
