Model scenarios

This is how it works in practice. Three situations that happen in teams all the time — and one way to finally name and resolve them.

Scenario 01

"We can't keep up"

The team consistently misses deadlines. Everyone knows, but nobody knows why.

"We need more time." / "We have too many tasks."

Safety · fear of mistakes · unclear responsibility

Nobody knows who is allowed to make decisions. So nobody decides — and everyone waits.

Clarifying authority + reducing the penalty for mistakes → faster decision-making, end of paralysis.

Scenario 02

"We need to have everything under control"

The manager micromanages. The team is frustrated, the manager is overloaded.

"I'd rather check that myself." / "I want to know about everything."

Control as protection of stability · distrust of the process (not of people)

There is no shared definition of "done". Everyone has a different one — the manager doesn't know how to notice without constant monitoring.

Transparent checkpoints instead of ongoing oversight → greater autonomy, lower manager overload.

Scenario 03

"Nobody says anything"

Meetings run smoothly. Everyone agrees. But behind closed doors, everything is questioned.

"OK, let's move on." / "Sure, that makes sense."

Relationship · preserving harmony · psychological safety

People are afraid that disagreement will damage the relationship or their position. Silent agreement is protection, not conviction.

"What could go wrong?" as a standard question at every meeting → risks caught early, more open communication.
